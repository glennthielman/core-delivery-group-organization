Enterprises outside the technology sector that run a nearshore arrangement, and what can actually be established about how they run it. Belgian or Belgium-anchored where possible.

The test applied in [[References]] is whether a framing acts on the problems in [[Working structure - core and delivery group]]. The test applied here is narrower and harder: **what is verifiable**. Every entry below separates what the organisation has published from what is inferred, and marks recruitment copy as recruitment copy. Nothing in this note should reach [[Talk deck - Taylorism and Druckerism]] in a stronger form than it is stated here.

---

## The headline finding

No Belgian enterprise outside the technology sector has published a method for continuously delivering value through a nearshore arrangement. The search for one returns three categories of material, none of which is a method: vendor marketing, press releases announcing that a centre has opened, and job postings.

The shape of that absence is itself the argument the talk makes. Every organisation below publishes its **input** figures in detail — headcount, hiring targets, square metres, founding years, cities. Ahold Delhaize announces two hundred and fifty people. KBC announces four hundred IT professionals in Varna. Barry Callebaut announces seven hundred in Łódź. Not one publishes what the money bought. The most candid artefact found in the whole search is a first-anniversary write-up of Ahold Delhaize's Bucharest hub that reports "rapid growth and impactful projects" and names no product, no feature and no measurable outcome.

This is the degraded-Taylorism reading appearing unprompted in the public record: **input is measured precisely because it arrives on an invoice, output is not measured at all, and what is reported in place of output is a count of the input.** The drift is gradient rather than intent — a headcount is trivially countable and an outcome is genuinely hard — but the result is that the only publicly stated measure of a nearshore arrangement is its size.

Two consequences for the talk. First, this is defensible on stage at no cost, because it cites only what the organisations themselves chose to publish; it makes no claim about whether any of them is succeeding. Second, it supplies a cleaner route into the offshore material than an argument about distance: the question is not whether the arrangement works but whether anyone has established what it produces.

## KBC — Brno, Varna, and a stated nearshoring method

### What is verifiable

KBC operates captive service centres rather than contracting a supplier. The Czech entity dates from 2011 and the Bulgarian entity from 2019, with an IT Hub added in Varna in 2021. KBC's own careers material states that the Varna IT Hub delivers "software development, AI, QA and other IT solutions" alongside back-office work in daily banking, credits, AML, due diligence, insurance, payments and securities services.

Two claims of more interest appear in recruitment material rather than in corporate publication, and have to be carried as such. The first is that IT teams work to SAFe, combining software engineers, scrum masters, functional analysts and programmers. The second, and the reason this entry is first: KBC's Shared Services & Operations describes itself as a competence centre offering **"a proven standardized methodology, tools and governance as a service"** to internal KBC departments taking their first steps in nearshoring, with an Incubator team that onboards activities from other group departments.

### What is not published

Whether the methodology works, what it contains, how work is defined at the boundary, and what any of it produced. The headcount figures that circulate — 1500+ across Brno and Prague, 1000+ in Varna of whom 400+ are IT — come from job boards and regional business press, not from KBC.

### What it is good for

This is the closest thing in the search to the structure proposed in [[Working structure - core and delivery group]] existing in the wild. A nearshoring methodology offered as a service to internal clients, with a named team owning it, is recognisably an interface with an owner — the thing section 11 argues has to exist on day one. It is worth citing for the shape of the idea, phrased as *KBC describes* rather than *KBC does*.

### Sources

- [KBC Global Services — About Us](https://careers.kbc-group.com/SSC_BG/content/About-Us/?locale=en_GB) — KBC's own page. Founding years and service scope; the only primary source in this entry.
- [KBC IT Hub, Brno](https://careers.kbc-group.com/SSC_CZ/content/KBC-IT-Hub/?locale=en_GB) — KBC careers.
- [KBC Group opens a Digital Development Center in Varna](https://amcham.bg/2021/04/15/kbc-group-opens-a-digital-development-center-in-varna/) — AmCham Bulgaria, April 2021. Announcement of the second Varna centre and the initial hiring target.
- [Project Associate (INU), KBC Shared Services & Operations](https://europeanjobdays.eu/en/node/286883) — job posting. Sole source for the methodology-as-a-service and Incubator description. Recruitment copy; weak evidence, interesting claim.

## Euroclear — Kraków

### What is verifiable

Euroclear, headquartered in Brussels and not a technology company, runs an engineering presence in Kraków organised into tribes and squads, with roles posted for engineering squad leads, platform engineers, product owners and cloud engineers. The stated work includes migration of on-premises applications to Azure and a customer onboarding tribe delivering digital customer experience.

### What is not published

Everything else. There is no corporate statement of the operating model; the entire picture is assembled from job advertisements, which describe the organisation as it wishes to be read by candidates.

### What it is good for

Little on its own. Its value is corroborative: a second Brussels-headquartered financial institution, independently, running captive engineering in central Europe inside a tribe-and-squad structure rather than a supplier contract. It strengthens the pattern in the closing section and should not be cited for anything more.

### Sources

- [Engineering Squad Lead, Kraków](https://justjoin.it/job-offer/euroclear-engineering-squad-lead-krakow-net) — job posting.
- [Senior Cloud Software Engineer, Kraków](https://justjoin.it/job-offer/euroclear-senior-cloud-software-engineer-krakow-testing) — job posting.

## Ahold Delhaize — AD/01, Bucharest

### What is verifiable

AD/01 launched in April 2024 in Bucharest as a tech studio serving Ahold Delhaize's European brands, with a stated target of 250 technology employees over several years. Work named at launch: web and application development, e-commerce, data, loyalty, infrastructure. By its first anniversary the hub reported over 200 engineers against a target of 236 by the end of 2025, spanning software engineering, cloud, data analytics, quality assurance, product management and design, with a second hub announced for Belgrade.

### What is not published

How the teams are organised, how work reaches them, and what was delivered. The first-anniversary write-up names no product and no measurable outcome. Note also what the composition does say: product management and design sit inside the hub, not only engineering — which is a structural fact about where definition lives, and the single most useful detail in the entry.

### What it is good for

The freshest example, and the one with a Belgian thread that does not require naming an industry. It is also the cleanest illustration of the headline finding: a hub whose first year is reported entirely in headcount. Useful on stage precisely because the reporting asymmetry is visible in two sentences.

### Sources

- [Ahold Delhaize opens tech studio in Romania](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/food/ahold-delhaize-opens-tech-studio-in-romania/) — RetailDetail, April 2024. Launch, target headcount, scope, leadership quote.
- [AD/01 reaches 200 employees](https://outsourcing-today.ro/?p=14324) — Outsourcing Today. First-anniversary figures, disciplines, Belgrade announcement, and the "rapid growth and impactful projects" formulation quoted above.
- [Ahold Delhaize newsroom announcement](https://aholddelhaize.com/news/ahold-delhaize-opens-a-tech-studio-in-bucharest-to-further-strengthen-its-digital-data-and-tech-capabilities) — the primary release. Did not resolve when checked on 11 October 2026; cited for completeness, not relied on.

## Barry Callebaut — Łódź

### What is verifiable

A shared services centre established in Łódź in 2015, covering finance, IT, customer service, logistics, HR, master data and quality. Growth from roughly 270 employees to around 700, with an expansion announced in October 2020 adding at least 100 jobs across finance, IT, logistics, transport and quality, and a transformation into a Global Business Services centre in 2025.

### What is not published

Whether any software is developed there as opposed to operated, and in what arrangement. "IT" in a shared services context frequently means support and operations rather than development, and nothing found distinguishes the two.

### What it is good for

One thing, and it is worth having: an industry as far from software as chocolate, running the identical structure. It is the entry that stops the pattern from reading as a finance-sector phenomenon, which matters given that the rest of the Belgian material is banks. Cite it for the fact of the arrangement, not for a method.

### Sources

- [Barry Callebaut SSC Europe](https://focusonbusiness.eu/en/companies/barry-callebaut-ssc-europe/1017) — Focus on Business. Founding, scope, headcount trajectory, GBS transition.
- [Barry Callebaut shared services expansion](https://apps.eurofound.europa.eu/restructuring-events/detail/102543) — Eurofound restructuring database, October 2020. The 100-job expansion, recorded independently of company communications.

## Cegeka and Fluvius — the only described working arrangement

### What is verifiable

Cegeka, a Belgian supplier, consolidated its nearshore activity into a European Delivery Center spanning Romania, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Moldova, acquiring the Moldovan developer DAS Solutions and basing the cluster on Bucharest and Iași. The Fluvius digital-meter data platform is described as having been built with Cegeka **as a single team**, using an agile approach.

### What is not published

What "a single team" means operationally — shared backlog, shared definition of done, shared on-call, or co-located ceremonies with separate accountability. The description is Cegeka's own, published as a customer success story, which is the weakest evidentiary class in this note.

### What it is good for

It is the only Belgian material found that describes a *working arrangement* rather than a location, which is why it is here despite its provenance. Treat it as an illustration of the vocabulary organisations reach for — "one team" — and note what that vocabulary conspicuously omits: who may refuse a package, and who is accountable for the outcome. That omission is the gap [[Working structure - core and delivery group]] fills with an explicit *not accountable for* list.

### Sources

- [Cegeka European Delivery Center](https://outsourcing-today.ro/?p=5758) — Outsourcing Today. The nearshore cluster and its locations.
- [Cegeka strengthens regional presence](https://www.romania-insider.com/index%2ephp/cegeka-takeover-moldova-oct-202) — Romania Insider. The Moldova acquisition.
- [Cegeka customer cases](https://www.casestudies.com/company/cegeka) — vendor-published. Source of the Fluvius "single team" description.

## ING — not Belgian, best documented

### What is verifiable

ING Hubs Romania states that it delivers 130 services to 24 ING units worldwide with over 2000 engineering, risk and operations staff, organised into squads with chapter leads and an explicitly Spotify-derived model, running stand-ups, refinements, retrospectives and marketplaces. Named squads own end-to-end delivery of a product across many countries — the payments example cited covers twenty countries and three regional hubs. ING Tech Poland operates on the same pattern.

### What is not published, and what is

Unusually for this note, a fair amount *is*: ING's agile reorganisation has been written up externally at length, which makes it the only entry where the method can be examined rather than inferred. What remains unpublished is the same thing missing everywhere else — an outcome measure. The published material describes structure, not what the structure produced.

### What it is good for

Two uses. As the strongest available example of a non-technology enterprise running nearshore engineering as a single organisation rather than a supplier relationship, and as a caution: ING resolved the problem this structure addresses largely by **owning the people**. A squad with a chapter lead and an end-to-end product mandate is not a delivery group receiving packages; the boundary has been dissolved rather than engineered.

That distinction is worth making explicitly on stage. It is also close enough to Belgium — ING Belgium — to land in a Brussels room without pretending to be a Belgian case.

### Sources

- [ING Hubs Romania](https://careers.ing.com/es/romania-hubs) — ING's own page. Service count, unit count, headcount.
- [Technical Product Owner, Business Banking, ING Hubs RO](https://careers.ing.com/es/trabajo/bucarest/technical-product-owner-business-banking-ing-hubs-ro/3121/20976442048) — job posting. Squad scope and ceremony detail.

## Colruyt — the useful counter-example

### What is verifiable

Colruyt Group states that it builds a large part of its systems, applications and solutions entirely in-house and tailored to its needs, working with partners only for what it does not develop itself, with around 2000 IT staff across Belgium, France and India.

### Why it belongs here

It is the one Belgian enterprise in the search whose published position is an explicit claim about *where the building happens* rather than about where people sit. That is a statement about method, however brief, and it is the opposite choice from every other entry. India is also offshore rather than nearshore, so it does not answer the question as posed — but it marks the boundary of the pattern and prevents the closing section from reading as inevitability.

### Sources

- [Colruyt Group IT](https://www.colruytgroup.com/en/about-us/support-activities/it/it) — Colruyt's own page.

## The pattern that holds

Across every case that looks like it works, the distinguishing variable is neither methodology nor geography. It is **employment and single-process membership**: a captive centre, directly employed staff, one backlog, one definition of done, and people carrying titles like product owner and chapter lead rather than receiving specifications as a supplier. The arrangements with nothing to show beyond headcount are, without exception, the contracted ones.

Supporting research points the same way, and is of a better evidentiary class than anything above. Work on governance-mode selection for knowledge-intensive offshoring finds that cost-reduction motives specifically push firms toward the vendor model, while market-seeking motives push toward the captive centre; separate work using the Spanish Technological Innovation Panel finds captive offshoring has a greater effect on innovation outcomes than offshore outsourcing, with the effect stronger on product than process innovation. Stated plainly: **the cheaper governance mode is the one that produces less, and cost pressure is what selects it.** Forrester figures cited by ISG put the cost gap per person per month at roughly 17 per cent, while noting that captive centres staff work with people carrying eight to nine years of experience where vendors would assign two to three.

This both supports and sharpens the design in [[Working structure - core and delivery group]]. The published successes mostly did not solve the interface problem; they bought the people and made the interface internal. That option is not available here, which makes this the harder case rather than the weaker one: the structure has to produce the correct outcome **without** changing the employment relationship, by making the envelope equal the correct outcome rather than by dissolving the boundary. Worth saying out loud on stage, because it pre-empts the obvious question from the floor — *why not just insource?* — and answers it with a reason rather than a shrug.

One caution that follows directly from [[Two profiles in a software team]] and from the design decisions in the working structure: none of this licenses treating the captive model as the mature form and the contracted model as the immature one. The arrangement is a consequence of cost pressure and of what an organisation is willing to carry on its own payroll, not of anyone's competence. The classification in the working structure sorts by work class, never by employer — and this note is exactly the sort of material that could be misread into sorting by employer if it is summarised carelessly.

## What could not be found

Recorded so the search is not repeated from scratch.

- Any Belgian non-technology enterprise publishing an operating model for nearshore software delivery — definition standards, acceptance criteria, routing rules, anything of the kind set out in [[Working structure - core and delivery group]].
- Any published outcome measure for a nearshore arrangement at any of the organisations above.
- Any case study of a Belgian bank or insurer combining a scaled-agile transformation with nearshore delivery. [Belfius' SAFe transformation](https://www.apptio.com/case-study/scaling-agile-belfius-bank/) is documented, but without a nearshore component; Argenta's is attributed only through a consultant biography.
- Any independent, non-vendor account of a nearshore arrangement succeeding or failing on stated criteria.

The first and fourth are the ones worth re-checking if a Belgian enterprise ever publishes a technology-organisation write-up. Everything else here is unlikely to change.

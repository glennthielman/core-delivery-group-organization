A talk for people who build software inside large organisations

Counting the forest,
by measuring the trees

Two ways a software team can run, and a working structure for when both have to share one organisation.

Open cold, no agenda slide. The title is the whole argument in one line: a precise count of every tree is not a view of the forest. Hold the title for a beat before moving on.
The title

Every tree is counted. The forest is inferred.

The trees
hours bookedheadcountday ratesutilisationstory pointstickets closed

Measured precisely, reported on a schedule, reconciled monthly.

The forest

What did all of it change, for anyone outside the team?

Not measured. Assumed from the trees.

?

The first half of this talk names how that happens. The second half is a structure that measures the forest.

The metaphor is used sparingly from here. Trees are the units that arrive on invoices and in trackers. The forest is the outcome. Nothing here is a criticism of counting; the question is what the count is mistaken for.
1

Two profiles

What each looks like when it is already running inside a team, observed in a week.

Part 1 runs roughly sixteen minutes. Goal: recognition. The audience should be able to name what is happening to them, and nobody should leave feeling that one profile is the villain.
Vantage point

An outsider, five days, no questions about beliefs

Allowed to watch:

  • the board
  • the meetings
  • the chat
  • the way people talk about each other

Not allowed to ask what the team believes about management.

Neither profile is a job title or a methodology

Nobody in either team describes themselves in these terms.

Both can wear the same framework

Same ceremonies, same tooling, same board, same words.

This framing keeps the discussion on observable structure rather than on intentions or beliefs. It also pre-empts the objection that a team "does Scrum properly": both profiles can.
Profile one

The Taylorist team: a stage in a pipeline

Shape

  • Boundaries drawn around activity types: the API team, the build squad, the test function
  • Asked who the customer is, the answer names an internal party
  • Each function has its own queue and manager

How work arrives

  • Pre-decomposed. The problem has already become a solution, and the ticket describes the solution
  • "Add field X to endpoint Y", not "the customer needs to see Z"
  • Refinement exists to make work ready for execution without needing context
  • The ticket is written by one person, executed by another, and the writer is more senior
Every item here is observable in a week. The question "what is the task?" was answered off-stage, before the work arrived. What remains on the team's side is "how long will it take?"
Profile one

What is counted, and how it sounds

Measurement

  • Counts units per period: points, tickets, pull requests, commits
  • Estimates compared to actuals; the gap is treated as estimation accuracy, not as information about the work
  • A chart goes up and to the right and has nothing to do with anything outside the team
  • Utilisation is tracked; idle time is waste
  • Measurement flows upward, for control

Vocabulary

resourceFTEheadcountvelocityutilisationburn-downhand-offsign-offgatereadyin scope / out of scopechange request"the business"

Heard in the corridor:

"just implement the ticket""that is not my ticket""who is assigned?"
Vocabulary gives it away fastest. Note the pattern of people referred to by count and skill class ("two more mediors") rather than by name and strength.
Profile one

Strengths and costs

Strengths

It does not depend on exceptional people. That is why organisations at scale converge on it.

  • Predictable: standard work, comparable output, plannable
  • Fast to staff: the method lives in artefacts, so a newcomer is productive without years of context
  • Clear boundaries: everyone knows what is theirs
  • A genuine fit for people whose best work is a bounded problem with a clear path
Costs
  • The outcome depends on the quality of a definition made upstream, by someone who is not doing the work
  • Context is lost at each hand-off; the people closest to the code have the least say
  • Deviation needs permission; improvement arrives as a programme
  • Without an output measure, shortfalls stay invisible until undeniable
The decisive strength is stated plainly, because it is true and because it explains the gravitational pull. A structure that needs no heroes survives turnover, scale and a bad quarter.
Profile two

The Druckerian team: owner of an outcome

Shape

  • Organised around an outcome someone outside the organisation experiences
  • Small and mixed: the disciplines needed to go from problem to running software
  • Nobody's job title fully predicts what they will do this week
  • People can name the customer, and some have spoken to one recently

How work arrives

  • As a problem or a desired outcome; how is open when work is picked up
  • The team is expected to come back and say "this is not worth doing" or "the real problem is elsewhere"
  • The team defines the task, out loud, about its own work
  • Method sits with the people doing the work
Mirror of slide 5. Drucker's first question, "what is the task?", is asked by the team about its own work. Seniority shows up as more context and better questions, not as the right to specify.
Profile two

What is counted, and how it sounds

Measurement

  • Counts what happened outside the team: users, errors in production, latency, adoption, support load, time from idea to live
  • Measurement flows to the doer, for self-correction, fast enough to act on
  • Forecasts exist where a date matters, and are revised without blame
  • Nobody tracks individual utilisation; slack is unremarkable

Vocabulary

outcomecustomerthe problem we are solvingcontributionownershipwe decidedwe were wrong aboutstop doingdrop it

Heard in the room:

"what are we actually trying to do here?""would we start this today?""ask Sara, she has been through this"
Absences are as telling as presences: no "resource", no "the business" as a distant actor, little talk of scope boundaries. People are referred to by name and by strength.
Profile two

Strengths and costs

Strengths
  • Finds the real problem: stopping or redirecting work is a normal weekly act
  • Method adapts as fast as the work changes
  • Feedback reaches the person who can act on it, quickly
  • Context stays in people, so edge cases are handled where they arise
  • The profile for investigation: work whose answer is not known in advance
Costs
  • Depends on people with judgement, context and the habit of speaking up; real key-person risk
  • Hard to scale and hard to replace: when someone leaves, the work has to be re-thought
  • Hard to forecast for finance; slack looks like waste to anyone counting utilisation
  • Roles blur: technically-titled people carry communication, reporting and planning nobody assigned them
  • Outcome measures must be built, and can still be turned into targets
Costs are stated with the same weight as the Taylorist ones. The key-person risk is real and returns in Part 2 as a known cost of the core team. Deming's point on numerical goals applies to outcome measures as much as to any other.

The sharpened thesis

Taylorism with the knowledge half removed

Taylor's knowledge claim

How long a task takes is a measurable fact, discoverable by investigation.

Study the work. Time it. Establish what is possible. Then both sides look at the same number.

Dropped.

Taylor's control apparatus

Standards, instruction cards, planning separated from execution, functional supervision.

Everything needed to direct work from above, assuming the number underneath is real.

Kept.

The organisation pays all of Taylorism's costs and collects none of its benefit.

Large enterprises did not choose Taylorism; they grew into it. They describe themselves in Drucker's vocabulary, believe it, and keep asking for innovation the structure has no path to produce. This is the pivot of Part 1.
What gets measured

Input to the hour. Output not at all.

Input: precise, current
headcountday rateshours bookedutilisationbudget consumed

It arrives on invoices, so finance needs it.

Output: not established

What the money bought is recorded nowhere.

What looks like output measurement

The team's estimate of its own work, compared against itself. The line can rise while nothing outside changes.

One column is precise to the hour; the other had to be reconstructed from ticket histories. Velocity is not dishonest, it is self-referential: it can rise indefinitely without touching anything outside.
How it happens

A gradient, not malice

Input cost is trivially countable
→
Output is genuinely hard
→
Measurement descends to the easy half
→
No early signal

Second-order effect

With no output measure, problems stay invisible until they are catastrophic. A manager who raises a concern with no metric behind it is simply the person bringing bad news.

So silence is rational

Until the problem is undeniable, nobody is rewarded for being early. It is a property of the structure, not of anyone's character.

Taylor is a critic of these organisations, not their patron. His 1900 diagnosis applies unchanged: management did not actually know how long the work should take.

This rescues the Taylor history from being a villain origin story. Taylor started from the premise that the time a task takes is a fact to be investigated. The enterprise kept the control and dropped the investigation.
Preference, not type

The thought was there. The forum was wrong.

Structural half · changeable
  • The forum: open meetings collect nothing
  • The incentive: asking has a social cost, guessing has none
  • Change both; expect it to take months
Preference half · design around it
  • People genuinely differ in which profile they thrive in
  • Bounded problems with clear paths suit some people, and are where they do their best work
  • A working preference and a strength, never a grade of engineer

The person who never volunteers an opinion in an open forum gives a precise, considered answer when asked directly.

Careful with register: this slide is where the talk could slide into sorting people. Keep it to forum and incentive on one side, working preference on the other. Preference is designed around, not corrected.
When both share one organisation

Premises for the design, taken from experience

Defined work only

Delivers what is explicitly defined, and nothing that is not. Room for investigation produces no usable result.

Opinions on request

Ask a person and the answer is considered. Do not ask and there is silence, even in meetings convened to collect opinions.

Disengaged, not disenfranchised

Real say over craftsmanship. What is absent is any wish to participate beyond the ticket.

Distance is not it

The loop is short and questions can be answered the same morning. They are not asked. The barrier is social, not temporal.

Attribution, not investigation

Nobody says they were wrong. With no shared record of what was supposed to happen, there is nothing to compare against but each other.

Documentation is a maze

Extensive and unusable. Finding the page is harder than asking, and asking is not done either.

Carry-over is never read

Remaining points roll sprint after sprint. The data is not hidden. It is never looked at.

Stable horizon

Broader responsibility was offered over several years and not taken up. This will not change within the planning horizon.

These are premises, not findings to re-argue. Everything is structural: nothing here is an explanation by culture. The design that follows holds whether the stream is staffed by an external provider, an internal team or AI agents; an agent that proceeds on a plausible guess rather than asking is the same failure shape.
Bridge
Stop trying to make a delivery group exceed the envelope.
Make the envelope equal the correct outcome.

If the minimum is sufficient, minimum-effort behaviour stops being a problem.

This sentence is the hinge between the halves. Everything in Part 2 is a consequence of it. Pause here; this is the end of Part 1.
2

The working structure

A core team and delivery streams, working side by side while the landscape keeps evolving.

Part 2 runs roughly twenty-four minutes. Goal: a specific operating model, in depth. The core team is a proposal, not the current state; there is no core team today.
The organising principle
Definition completeness is the interface, and it is the core team's product.

If delivery executes exactly the envelope it is given, the envelope determines the outcome. A shortfall in delivery is a shortfall in the envelope. That puts the defect with the party that can fix it.

Ambiguity yields nothing
→
Ambiguity never crosses the boundary
The minimum is what gets delivered
→
The minimum is made identical to the correct outcome
Documentation outgrows navigation
→
Rules are executable, not written
Nothing is volunteered
→
Information is pulled on a schedule
Blame has nowhere useful to go
→
Accountability is defined so precisely the question does not arise
Left column is the observed premise; right column is the design response. Each of the next slides expands one of these. This is also the table of contents for Part 2.
Where the boundary actually falls

The design sorts by work class, never by location or employer

Core team

Holds the context, the access and the authority to change the landscape. Does Class C work.

Delivery streams

Execute classified, fully defined work. A stream can be an external provider, an internal team, or AI development agents.

Both populations typically sit in the same country, sometimes in the same room. Some of the most engaged people may be in delivery.

The failure mode

"Class A" becomes shorthand for work we send over there. Classification stops being about the work, and the conflict the structure prevents returns by another door.

The corollary

Someone twenty metres away who prefers bounded work with a clear path is served well by the same structure. A complete package is a better experience for anyone who works that way.

Worth adopting even if the current arrangement ends tomorrow.

One of three decisions that are easy to undo by accident. If an audience member hears "Class A" as "work we send over there", stop and correct it. The design is not a position for or against any particular delivery group arrangement, and it is not contingent on who staffs a stream.
The whole picture

Work is classified before it crosses the boundary

Walk the diagram left to right once. Class C stays with the core team. Class B goes through a definition step and becomes A. Only Class A crosses the boundary. Fitness functions and the gates are the same pipeline for both sides. The dotted line is production signal feeding landscape work.
Two fatal misreadings

It sounds like splitting the team, and like writing better tickets

Not a reorganisation

No reporting line changes. Nobody moves desk, manager or employer.

Not better tickets

The ticket is a by-product. The product is the landscape and the scaffolding around it.

Not more process

On balance it removes steps: the re-explaining, the rework loop, the meeting about who was responsible.

Not a transformation programme

No name, no rollout, no training. It starts on one stream and is judged on that stream's own numbers.

Not contingent on anyone else changing

No renegotiation, no different hiring, no change in anyone's willingness. It works with the arrangement that exists.

Not anti-agile. Not a position on who should do the delivery.

Heard as a reorganisation it triggers resistance; heard as better tickets it triggers "a layer of cost proposed by people who want more time to write things down". Neither is what this is.
1 · Work classification

Nothing crosses the boundary unclassified

Class A · Defined

The solution is known. An existing pattern applies. The blast radius is bounded and named. The acceptance cases can be enumerated.

→ Routes to delivery

Class B · Definable

The outcome is understood but the solution is not yet worked out. The core team can resolve it in bounded time.

→ Definition step, becomes A, then routes

Class C · Investigation

The outcome, the solution or the blast radius is unknown. Needs exploring the landscape, reading unfamiliar code, or an architectural decision.

→ Core team only. Never routes

Classification is the scarcest skill in the arrangement and in nobody's job description by default. Name it, and make someone answerable for it.

Classification is done by the core team. It appears in nobody's job description, which is why it silently defaults to whoever is under the most schedule pressure unless it has an owner.
1 · The routing rule

Five questions. No exceptions.

Is the outcome known?
→
no: C
Is the solution known, with an existing pattern?
→
no: bounded time? B or C
Is the blast radius bounded and named?
→
no: C
Can the acceptance cases be enumerated?
→
no: B
All yes: Class A. Routes to delivery.

Why there are no exceptions

Routing B or C work across the boundary produces three failures at once:

  • no usable output
  • a schedule that slips late and invisibly
  • a conflict where delivery is blamed for something it was never equipped or asked to do

Every "they did the absolute minimum" traces back to a package that was not Class A when it was sent.

This is the single most important rule in the structure. Under any schedule pressure, work crosses only as Class A. The cost of the rule is an empty queue now and then, which is agreed in advance.
2 · The work package

Every field is required, or it is not Class A

Outcome

What becomes true, and why. The only thing that exposes an internally contradictory package.

Scope

Modules, services and files that may be touched, named explicitly.

Out of scope

What must not be touched. Silence reads as permission or prohibition depending on the day.

Reference implementation

A link to real code that compiles and runs. Not a prose description.

Acceptance

The cases, enumerated.

Non-functionals

Only those that apply, from the automated gates. Or "all automated".

When blocked

Who to contact, through which channel, and the response commitment.

Every field fillable?
Release as Class A
An unfillable field?
Not Class A. Back to definition.

That is the gate doing its job, not a failure.

The package determines the outcome, so it gets a mandatory structure. The gate runs on the core team's side: an unfillable field is discovered at definition time, by the person who can resolve it, instead of mid-implementation by someone who will not ask.
2 · Acceptance scaffolding

Pre-written failing tests make minimum effort and correct outcome the same thing

The strongest definition is a set of failing tests committed with the package. But it cannot be afforded as a universal rule, and a design that depends on it is abandoned in a month. The resolution is to split the two halves of a test.

The specification

Which cases, and what is expected in each.

Where the judgement lives, and it is cheap. The core team owns it; it is the thinking that goes into acceptance criteria today, written down differently.

The fixtures and plumbing

Setup, data, assertions.

Where the cost lives, and it is mechanical. Handed to whoever writes the code, and progressively automated. Built once, reused across every package, so the cost falls over time.

Per-package cost cannot be afforded; per-landscape cost amortises. The same economics run through the whole structure. It is also the only form of definition that answers a question nobody has to ask, which matters most where the question will not be asked.

2 · The ladder

Level is chosen per package, by risk. Default to level 2.

LevelThe package carriesCore-team costUse for
1Prose acceptance criteriaminutesNothing. The current state, and the problem
2Case table, verifiable by developer and QA5–15 minThe default for everything
3Case table plus test names, no bodies+10 minShared code; real blast radius
4Pre-written failing testshoursMoney movement, compliance logic, migrations that cannot be redone

Why level 1 persists

It reads as complete to whoever wrote it, who has the cases in their head. The reader supplies a different set and both leave believing they agree. "Done" becomes an opinion settled by seniority.

A diagnostic

If level 4 starts to feel necessary for most packages, the problem is upstream: the work is not really Class A, or the harness is too weak to trust levels 2 and 3.

Level 2 relies on the second reader: the criteria must be verifiable by a QA or analyst, not only by the developer, so the dual-verification rule is the control. Level 4 is the only fully self-verifying level, and also costs the core team a share of the work twice.
2 · Level 3, and a note on generation

Test names without bodies turn the cases into structure

void appliesFeeToInvoiceOverdueByThirtyDays()
void doesNotApplyFeeBeforeThirtyDays()
void doesNotApplySecondFeeInSameMonth()
void doesNotApplyFeeToZeroBalance()
void logsWarningForClosedAccount()

No fixtures, no assertions. The cases are committed, appear in test reports, and a missing or empty test is visible at review. "Did they handle the already-charged case?" becomes something the build answers.

Generation splits on the right seam

A human supplies the cases, where judgement lives. A model supplies the fixtures, where the cost lives. If that holds, level 4 becomes the default.

The limit

A generated test that encodes the wrong expectation is worse than no test. It looks authoritative and turns a silent assumption into a permanent one. The case table stays human-written and human-reviewed.

Adopting level 2 now is not a stopgap. It is the input the real solution needs.

Scaffolding investments in order of return: test harness, slice test templates, contract-first OpenAPI, reference implementations worth copying, and AI context committed to the repository. The last costs a file and improves output for anyone using assistance on either side of the boundary.

3 · A Spring landscape

The core team changes the landscape by changing the fitness functions

@AnalyzeClasses(packages = "com.example.billing")
class LandscapeRules {

    @ArchTest
    static final ArchRule web_never_reaches_persistence =
        noClasses().that().resideInAPackage("..web..")
            .should().dependOnClassesThat()
            .resideInAPackage("..persistence..");

    @ArchTest
    static final ArchRule state_changes_are_audited =
        methods().that().areAnnotatedWith(StateChanging.class)
            .should().beAnnotatedWith(Audited.class);
}
  • ArchUnit: the highest-value item. Architectural intent as a failing test
  • Contract tests generated from OpenAPI
  • Flyway migration conventions
  • Checkstyle, Spotless, SpotBugs configured once, inherited
  • Coverage and mutation gates where meaningful
  • Compliance checks as tests: audit trail, logging, residency, transaction boundaries

Compliance is Class C by construction. Investigate once, encode the answer as a gate. It cannot be forgotten, skipped under deadline, or missed by someone who never found the page.

The code is illustrative; StateChanging and Audited are placeholders for whatever the landscape uses. A violation surfaces in minutes, in the developer's own editor, with no one to approach and nothing to look up. Encoded compliance is also the most defensible answer available to an auditor.

4 · Three mechanisms

Landscape work is committed work with its own backlog

A defended capacity split

■ 50 landscape
■ 30 authoring
■ 20 blockers, review

Illustrative. The landscape share is committed, not what is left over.

A WIP cap on authoring

A maximum number of packages per period. At the cap, the queue is allowed to run dry.

Agree it with management in advance. The first empty queue will look like a failure. It is the mechanism working; the alternative is packages that are not Class A going out under pressure.

Evolution aimed at routability

Improve the landscape so that a larger share of future work is Class A.

Boundaries matter most: when a typical unit of work fits one module, packages are small and review is cheap. No definition quality compensates for boundaries that cut across the work.

The proportion of work that can be routed as Class A is the number that justifies the capacity, and the guard against building a framework nobody asked for: if a change does not raise it, it is interest, not landscape work.
5 · The interface between the teams

The obvious design is the wrong one

Push · the obvious design

An open channel with a response commitment.

Fails on the central premise: nothing is volunteered. An hour of latency is not what stops the question. It is not asked when the answer is thirty seconds away, because asking has a social cost and guessing does not.

Pull · the design

Specific questions to specific people, on a schedule.

Not "any blockers?" to a group, which reliably returns nothing, but "you picked this up on Tuesday: what was unclear in it?" to one person. Fifteen minutes per developer per week, by name, on the calendar.

What is optimised is not round-trip time. The loop is already short. It is silent assumptions: work that proceeds on a guess, and surfaces weeks later as rework.

An open channel with a stopwatch attached is the same invitation that has not worked for years. The design uses the behaviour that occurs, not the one that should. The same logic applies to any stream that proceeds on a plausible guess rather than asking.
5 · Four rules, the first one load-bearing

Every answer produces two outputs

1 · Information is pulled, on a schedule. Fixed cadence, by name, someone accountable.

2 · Blocking is expected behaviour, not an escalation. A blocked package is a defect found in the core team's product. Expect months before it holds.

3 · A named responder on a rota, not also authoring packages that day.

4 · Two outputs per answer: the answer, and a change to the template, reference or gate so the gap cannot recur.

Without the second output the same question arrives every month and both sides conclude the other is not learning. The pull cadence carries the load until the blocking rule takes hold. This is also the cheapest step in the sequence and does not depend on anything else.
6 · The working agreement

The decisive element is the list of what delivery is not accountable for

Core team is accountable for
  • completeness and correctness of every released package
  • classification
  • responding within the window
  • gates and reference implementations
  • fitness of the landscape
Delivery is accountable for
  • delivering the package as defined
  • answering directly when asked
  • reporting a blocker rather than working around it
  • changing nothing outside the stated scope
Delivery is explicitly NOT accountable for
  • investigating
  • deciding architecture
  • identifying requirements that were not stated
  • judging whether the work should be done at all
  • improving anything outside the package

Once the second list is written down, "they should have noticed" and "they did the bare minimum" are no longer available, because nobody was asked to notice.

Conflict comes from unstated expectations: one side expecting initiative the other has no mandate to provide. Passing the automated gates is deliberately absent from the list; it is not an agreement, it is a merge that does not happen. Anything the pipeline can enforce is enforced there.
6 · Defect routing

The package is the shared record

Defect found
→

→
No, not stated
Specification gap, logged against the package
→
Template or fitness function updated
Yes, stated but wrong
Implementation error, logged against delivery
→

Why finger-pointing happens now

With no shared record of what was supposed to happen, the only evidence is each party's account, and admitting error carries a cost with no benefit.

Why this works

"Was this covered by the package as written?" has an answer, in a document both sides saw beforehand. Nobody has to be persuaded to accept fault, because fault stops being the thing under discussion.

Specification gaps are logged against the package, never against the person who followed it. Related hygiene: do not ask delivery for estimates and then treat them as commitments, and do not measure them on outcomes they do not control.
7 · Step zero: the baseline

Before anything changes, record how the current arrangement behaves

Every objection to this structure can be answered with "can you give me measurements on that?", and the same question defeats the proposal just as easily. Nobody can say what the present arrangement produces.

One to two days of one person's time. All from data that already exists. Recorded before it is announced, because numbers people know are watched start moving for the wrong reasons.

Rules for the baseline

  • Existing data only. No new tooling, fields or reports
  • Retrospective, one fixed window, the same for every measure
  • By stream, never by person. Measures the interface
  • Never cut by location or employer
  • Where data is missing, the gap is the finding
  • Method published with the numbers
A baseline produced after the results are known invites the accusation that the window was chosen to flatter, and the classification sample cannot be done after the fact. If only a fifth of defects can be traced to the work that caused them, that is not a failed measurement; it is the thesis expressed as a number.
7 · The measures

Five pulled from the tools, one sampled by hand, one from finance

1 · Sprint carry-over

Committed work not completed; items carried two sprints or more. Recorded at every close, never looked at.

2 · Reopen rate

Items that moved backwards after review, test or done. Rework already visible in the tooling.

3 · Defects after release

Total only, plus the share traceable to a story at all.

4 · Definition changed after start

The closest proxy for a silent assumption. Connects directly to the principle.

5 · Lead time and review rounds

Median and 85th percentile, never the average. A long tail is definition finished during review.

6 · Classification sample

10 to 20 completed items classified A, B or C by two people independently, acceptance rated against the ladder.

7 · Input, same window

Hours and cost from finance. Output now sits beside input for the first time.

Cross-check

If B and C items account for most reopens, escapes and mid-flight changes, the case is made with the organisation's own data.

Good for order of magnitude and direction of travel, not for decimals. Not an assessment of any person, team or supplier, and not a target. A quarter later, rerun the same queries and compare each stream against its own baseline, never against other streams.
8 · What to measure from then on

Measure the interface, not the people

  • Package completeness defect rate: gaps found per package, after release
  • First-time-pass rate
  • Round trips per package
  • Blocked time: raised to resumed
  • Escape defects by category: specification gap or implementation error
  • Proportion of work classifiable as A
  • Lead time, package-ready to merged
  • Authoring ratio: core hours per package against delivery hours

A caution that applies to every item

These exist for the core team's own correction. The moment one becomes a performance target for anyone, it will be optimised directly and stop carrying information.

Proportion-of-work-Class-A is especially easy to improve by reclassifying rather than by improving the landscape. Deming's eleventh point: measure to understand variation, and do not set numerical goals for people.

Package completeness defect rate is the core team's primary quality metric and the health indicator for the whole arrangement. The authoring ratio feeds the next slide.
9 · Capacity

What to say when asked to scale: the answer is arithmetic

Core authoring and triage bandwidth
Delivery headcount
→
Capacity ceiling
→
Below the ceiling: added headcount becomes throughput
Above the ceiling: added headcount becomes queue, then pressure to release packages that are not Class A, then bad delivery and conflict

Measure the ratio and publish it. That volume requires a proportional increase in core authoring capacity, or the additional people will wait.

This is the cost of the structure turned into a planning tool. It also protects against the failure mode where the structure is blamed for the consequences of scaling past its ceiling.
10 · Review

Review must be as deterministic as the package

The pipeline checks first
  • style and structure
  • boundaries
  • coverage
  • contracts
  • compliance gates

Before a human looks.

A human reviews what automation cannot see
  • against a written checklist, not individual taste
  • disagreement about preference is resolved by adding a rule to the pipeline, not by a comment thread nobody will push back on
Otherwise review becomes the largest single generator of round trips and resentment. Short slide; keep moving.
11 · The core team does not exist yet

Forming it: small, drawn from the team already working that way

Where the people come from. The nucleus is whoever already defines its own tasks, argues about problems rather than responsibility, and can say who the work is for. Mostly a different mandate, not hiring. What happens to the work it does now has to be answered first.

Four to six people. Authoring and classification are the constraint, and adding people to a group that holds shared context has the usual cost.

What to protect it from. Being measured on authoring throughput, because that is the only part of its output anyone upstream can see. The conversation happens before the team starts.

Needed on day one, in order
1 · Authority to refuse a package.
Without it: a documentation function.
2 · Access to production signal.
Without it: loses the feedback, becomes an architecture function.
3 · A named owner for classification.
Without it: classification defaults to whoever has the most pressure.
The core team is a proposal, not the current state. A core team assembled from people who have only worked in the first profile will produce packages that reproduce it. Where it is drawn from matters more than its size.
11 · How the core team works internally

In the forest, not above it

A team whose own practice is document-first and handoff-based produces document-first, handoff-based artefacts. Within a year it becomes the architecture function this structure exists to replace.

In the code

Writes production code in the systems it writes packages for. A week with no commits and its model starts to diverge.

Pair and mob

The one effective mitigation of key-person risk. More documents is not it.

Test first

A team cannot write scaffolding for others if it does not write tests for itself.

Refactor continuously

Small steps, fitness functions as a ratchet. Strangler over replacement.

Simple design

If a change does not raise the share routable as Class A, it is interest, not landscape work.

Whole team

The analyst and QA sit inside it, in the room when criteria are written.

Sustainable pace

The bottleneck for everything. Burning it ends the arrangement, and shows up as quality decay.

Compliance lives here

Class C by construction. End state: the answer encoded as a fitness function.

The practices are the extreme programming ones, and they are load-bearing rather than decorative. "In the forest, not above it" carries the Andres-Beck and Beck asymmetry of decay: a forest degrades into desert without anyone deciding to let it. Same word as the title, different use: here the forest is the landscape the team has to stay inside.
12 · Known costs

Stated, so they are decisions rather than surprises

  • Delivery capacity is capped by core-team bandwidth and does not scale by hiring delivery
  • The core team is on the critical path for everything, with real key-person risk that risk or audit will eventually ask about
  • The capability gap widens over time, and the structure has no mechanism to close it
  • Nobody moves from one group to the other. The cost with an ethical dimension. Make it an explicit position, not an emergent one; even a token rotation path changes what it means
  • The queue will sometimes be empty, and that is agreed in advance
  • The investment lands a quarter before the return: the bill arrives first
Drucker advocated outsourcing precisely so that people doing support work would have somewhere to advance. A structure where one population permanently cannot enter the other reproduces the problem he was trying to solve. Name it.
13 · What it buys

The structure is a means. This is the end.

The organisation can say what the money bought

Output beside input for the first time. The benefit that makes the rest arguable with evidence.

Capacity lost to rework comes back

The largest line item nobody has a figure for, currently attributed to the team being slower than expected.

The landscape can change again

Committed capacity, its own backlog, and a number that says whether it is paying off.

Problems surface early

Reopens, escapes and carry-over are leading indicators, available weekly. A conversation on Tuesday, not a defect in November.

Conflict stops being generated

"They did the minimum" is replaced by a question with an answer both sides can see.

Documented rules become enforced rules

A control encoded as a gate is cheaper to evidence and stronger than a policy document.

Pair with the honest arithmetic: a core team of four to six from existing staff, a standing authoring load that caps throughput at a measurable ceiling, and about a quarter before any return is visible.
14 · Sequence

Roughly in dependency order, not a timeline

  1. 0 · Record the baseline, before anything is announced
  2. 1 · Form the core team; settle mandate, size and its current work
  3. 2 · Agree capacity split and authoring cap, including what an empty queue means
  4. 3 · Write the working agreement, both lists, circulate on both sides
  5. 4 · Define the three classes and the routing rule; name the classifier
  6. 5 · Put the pull cadence on the calendar, by name, with an owner
  1. 6 · Build the package template; run ten packages through it
  2. 7 · Put ArchUnit and the compliance checks into the pipeline
  3. 8 · Build one reference implementation, real, deployed, worth copying
  4. 9 · Move acceptance to case tables on one stream; compare against its own baseline
  5. 10 · Scaffolding in order: harness, slice templates, contract-first, references, AI context
  6. 11 · Track package completeness defects and share of Class A from the start

Steps 5 and 9 are the cheapest, and they start returning first.

Step 2 happens before the team starts, not at its first review. Step 5 does not depend on anything else on the list. Step 9 requires no tooling.
What makes it decidable
Record the baseline. Run one stream for a quarter. Compare that stream against its own prior numbers.

If the rework, the carry-over and the mid-flight definition changes have not moved, the argument is wrong, and the evidence will say so.

This is the answer to "can you give me measurements on it" and the first time the organisation can say what the work produced rather than only what it cost.
